# Placing the Apollo 15 30-track tapes on mission time

**The tested higher-recall transcription workflow projects to 39–66 hours on the RTX 4090, depending on speech density.** The PAO reel repeats 4.279 s of audio near 12:10. A constant-rate fit using only calls after that repeat predicted seven withheld late exchanges to 4.05 s median absolute error and 5.27 s worst error. The DVD has its own timing breaks. Distilled Whisper with a gain-assisted speech detector included 20 of 21 difficult Spur openings. Ungated Parakeet and acoustic review supply a second check for historical timing. [Pilot measurements and code](../research/mission_timing/README.md) · [Timing residuals after the repeat](images/pao_572aaa_after_repeat.png) · [Review table](../catalog/pao_572aaa_anchor_predictions.csv)

The 30-track, PAO recordings, television sound and DVD transfers each inherit an analog recording and playback history. The same distinctive utterance can establish that two recordings contain the same event. A difference in the spacing of two utterances establishes _relative_ drift between the recordings. Assigning that drift to a particular recording requires mission-time evidence. The proposed reference is the original transcript’s Ground Elapsed Time (GET), with an uncertainty estimated from the material. The intended result is a tape-to-GET map with a stated range of accuracy and coverage. It cannot recover the missing IRIG signal.

## What the pipeline already supplies

Stage 02 places each WAV on a common tape clock while retaining its acquisition origin. Stage 04 removes qualified crosstalk. Stage 05b applies the shared flutter map to all channels at the corresponding tape time. Start timing research from completed 05b WAVs and their adjacent review records. A match on CH14, for example, belongs at the file’s measured tape origin plus the local 05b time. A delayed monitor or echo is a separate question about when that content reached the channel. [Shared tape map guide](08_2026-11-15_shared_deflutter.md)

Stage 06 already surveys Quindars across channels, consolidates duplicate transmissions, estimates slow speed from tone frequency and integrates that speed into one elapsed-time map per tape. It selects smoothing using withheld ten-minute blocks of tones and holds the nearest estimated rate beyond tone coverage. Its validation measures frequency error. A separate test in seconds is needed to establish mission-time accuracy. The nominal tone generator frequency introduces a possible systematic bias. The optional clock-anchor input checks authenticated clock evidence against the curve. Transcript observations belong in a separate evidence record. The current archive inventory lists no published stage-06 release. ## How to interpret a speech timestamp

**1. Place the recording on the tape.** Stage 02 measured the offset remembered from flutter tracing. The recovery inventory records nonzero capture delays on 33 channels across seven tapes. For example, T510073 CH21–24 each have `delayed_frames = +7828` (0.9785 s at 8 kHz). On T510088 several channels have values near −11.87 s. Different digitizer start times are a possible explanation. The established fact is the measured relative placement. The implementation subtracts the signed delay from the acquisition start, then rebases all channels to the earliest corrected start. The published 05b review’s canonical start already incorporates that placement. For a pick in its WAV, add local elapsed seconds to that start exactly once. [Coordinate calculation](../scripts/30-track/05_deflutter/tape_coordinates.py) · [Capture-alignment recovery](08_2026-11-15_shared_deflutter.md#capture-alignment-and-selective-recovery)

**2. Place the main utterance relative to the same speech on a reference channel.** Use the project's signed convention, `stagger = channel main time − reference main time`. A positive value means speech appears later on that channel. For example, a hypothetical main utterance at 101.32 s with a measured +1.32 s stagger corresponds to 100.00 s on the reference in the same coordinate system. The earlier/later decision in stage 04 says which repeat to subtract within the target. Reuse its saved donor and main timing measurements as evidence candidates. Establish the channel assignment by confirming the same **main** utterance against a common reference in separated passages, preserving local departures from the nominal 1.3 s. A repeated match confined to one monitored feed may describe that feed’s routing. Record its scope before generalizing to other speech on the channel. [Stagger convention and evidence requirements](03_2026-11-15_crosstalk_research.md#preserve-channel-stagger-assignments-for-later-historical-alignment)

**3. Carry the measurement through the actual map.** If stage 04 measured stagger in canonical _pre-flutter_ seconds, take the two matched source positions and locate both on the post-05b timeline using that release’s shared map. Their difference is the stagger in post-05b seconds at that event. Subtract that difference from the observed post-05b speech position to obtain the reference-equivalent position for comparison with the transcript. Store both positions and propagate the uncertainty. A measurement already made on 05b uses its recorded domain directly. The physical tape map continues to use simultaneous tape motion across channels. This timestamp calculation belongs to the interpretation of speech and can be done without moving any audio.

**Pilot inventory check, November 22.** The published capture-recovery scope records T510092 and T510093 as reusable with no nonzero capture-delay entries. Across the 30 published stage-04 final manifests for each reel, the main-utterance stagger group is `unknown` for all 60 tracks. T510092 nevertheless has eight channels with an echo-removal direction and T510093 has six. Start by reconciling earlier saved measurements with those manifests. If membership remains unresolved, choose one clearly identified channel and a consistent feed as the relative reference, and fit the initial transcript comparison on that channel alone. A constant unknown delay on that path is absorbed in the fitted origin. Test its stability across the reel before interpreting drift. Add other channels to the fit once their reference relationship is supported. Relative zero acquires a mission epoch only through the transcript anchors.

## Build a searchable transcript index

1. **Use the original technical air-to-ground scans as the timing edition.** Start with the existing OCR and retain the printed day, hour, minute and second, speaker, words, volume and page for each timed transmission. Store normalized GET and normalized search text alongside the original reading. Use page-layout OCR when columns separate. Flag invalid digits, backward times, overlapping calls and untimed continuation lines. Verify every pilot anchor against the scan. A whole-second stamp records the precision of the printing. Its accuracy and which point in the transmission it denotes still need to be established. [Original transcript scans](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/a15trans.html) · [Existing technical transcript OCR](https://archive.org/download/Apollo15/AS15_TEC_djvu.txt)
2. **Keep the transcript collections identifiable.** Index PAO commentary for announcing and commentary-only passages. Index CM and LM onboard transcripts for recorded cabin dialogue, including material replayed later. The Flight Journal and Lunar Surface Journal supply corrected readings, speakers, context and media links. Associate them by chapter and transmission, retaining discrepancies and edition details. Separate mission speech from editorial interviews and annotations. Use original NASA text for the reusable corpus and obtain any needed permission before bulk copying corrected journal text. [Flight Journal](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap15fj/index.html) · [Lunar Surface Journal](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/a15.html) · [NASA transcript collections](https://www.nasa.gov/history/mission-transcripts-mercury-gemini-and-apollo/)
3. **Reuse prior indexing where possible.** Doug Oard’s Apollo 15 project brought the technical and onboard scans, both journals and EVA television onto a common viewing timeline. Inspect its data for page-to-time and media-coverage pointers. Trace each pointer to its source before treating it as a timing observation. [University of Maryland Apollo Transcript Viewer](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~oard/apollo/)

### When to reuse embedded text and when to run OCR

**Start with layout-preserving extraction from the original technical transcript PDF.** Eleven sampled pages spread across the 1,478-page file all returned text. On PDF page 787 (printed page 816, tape 94/32), the embedded text keeps Scott's TV call, the speaker and the timestamp on one row. It reads the hour as `O1` and the word “I'll” as “Till”. The scan clearly shows `06 01 26 36`, or GET 145:26:36. The following rover readout is a useful search clue even with nearby recognition errors. Preserve both PDF and printed page numbers. This spot check covers the technical edition; check the text layer of the PAO, CM and LM editions separately. [Original technical transcript, PDF page 787](https://apollojournals.org/alsj/a15/AS15_TEC.PDF#page=787)

**Use the Lunar Sample Compendium workflow on a representative comparison set.** At LSC revision `77bdc8c`, `scripts/convert_pdfs.py` uses local OXR-1.0 for whole PDFs and emits page coordinates and layout blocks. Its earlier `scripts/2_prepare_table_repairs.py` uses Chandra OCR 2 through LM Studio for selected table repairs. For Apollo, compare embedded extraction and existing Internet Archive OCR against OXR on the same pages, using LSC's page-level workflow for a bounded trial. Include clean, faded, misaligned and continuation pages as well as Spur. Try the LM Studio model on difficult rows where the readings disagree. Use the scan alone as input for that second reading and preserve illegible fields as unknown. The LSC results on lunar-sample tables do not establish accuracy on typewritten GETs.

**Choose by timing-field accuracy.** Hand-key a small sample from the scans and score exact day/hour/minute/second, speaker and correct association with the utterance, including dropped or invented rows. Record time per page and review effort. If embedded extraction works on clean pages, reserve vision OCR for failed pages and a random audit of apparently clean ones, which can reveal plausible but wrong digits. Expand OXR to an edition if it consistently recovers more correct rows at acceptable cost. Keep each raw reading, source checksum, page/row location, engine revision and reviewed correction distinguishable. Verify each timing anchor against its printed row even when both OCR engines agree. This reduces transcription errors while retaining the original whole-second timing precision.

## Find timing channels before identifying every controller loop

Search a window around Spur on **all 30 numbered channels**, including the one labelled IRIG. Compare short sections of PAO or journal audio using spectrogram fingerprints. Confirm a candidate using several consecutive phrases, their order, and a local band-limited correlation or spectrogram alignment. Search for speed variation in short windows. An unrelated passage from the same reel provides a false-match control. Weak matches can still identify a promising channel for listening. Short-query acoustic fingerprinting is an established approach worth testing on these narrowband, overlapping voices. [Panako methods paper](https://www.theoj.org/joss-papers/joss.04554/10.21105.joss.04554.pdf)

For broader channel identification, sample two minutes near the beginning, middle and end of each channel on the pilot reel. That is **three channel-hours** for a first census. Use speech recognition on speech-bearing samples that audio matching leaves ambiguous. Search multi-turn sequences against the technical transcript, allowing for callsign, numeral and OCR variants. Forced alignment can help locate words after the passage is identified. Measure processing time and usable matches per channel-hour before estimating an archive-wide transcription budget. [WhisperX methods paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/bain23_interspeech.pdf)

Keep two findings per channel: **what feed is audible** and **which console or loop the channel likely represents**. A controller monitoring the spacecraft can provide a good timing match. Identifying that controller requires additional evidence such as whom they address, who responds, distinctive internal calls, or agreement with reel documentation. Record channel number, original label, proposed identity, evidence times and confidence. Test a suspected label shift on both sides of the proposed shift and across reel changes. Leave ambiguous identities open. For timing, prefer clear direct calls and mark uplink, downlink, monitored copies and delayed repeats separately.

## Measure mission-time error and decide what to change

Treat the printed GETs as **noisy measurements of position over a long baseline**. Begin with roughly ±1 s as a working scale for an ordinary observation and estimate its actual scatter from verified held-out calls. Typing errors, uncertain line onsets and OCR errors can be much larger. For scale, a two-second difference between endpoint errors looks like a 3.3% rate error across one minute, but only 0.019% across three hours. Closely spaced calls are valuable for identifying a passage. Estimate speed from many exchanges spanning tens of minutes to hours. Leave the pacing inside a short exchange to the underlying audio and, where available, the tape’s flutter and Quindar measurements.

**Test for slow wow throughout the recording.** A reel can start and end on time while calls in the middle wander one or two seconds early, then late. The owner observed timing wander of this size on earlier missions' PAO recordings. Its magnitude, timescale and source on Apollo 15 are open questions. Fit an overall rate as a baseline and plot the remaining timing error across the full span of each source. Its slope shows a rate error; a reversal in slope shows where the relative speed changes sign about that baseline. On the 30-track, inspect this plot both before and after the shared Quindar map.

Where speech is frequent, collect a sequence of distinctive calls through the rise, turn and fall of a suspected excursion. Use cross-recording audio matches to measure relative movement more finely than the printed seconds permit. Use transcript anchors to judge which source needs a correction. If the sources disagree and the transcript cannot resolve which one wandered, report the relative movement and leave attribution open. Check abrupt steps for edits or feed changes. Fit a smooth, forward-moving time map whose rate can increase and decrease without a fixed period. Compare smoothing spans by withholding successive whole time blocks, including interior blocks around suspected turns. Keep a bend only if it predicts withheld calls better than the baseline across the excursion. Show gaps where the available calls cannot resolve the shape.

1. **Collect anchors across the reel.** Aim for at least six distinct exchanges spread across early, middle and late regions as an initial feasibility test. Seek more wherever a rate change is suspected. Reserve an entire region, ideally near one end, before fitting. Keep every copy of an exchange in the same group. For each anchor retain the 05b review/map identity, channel, local sample, canonical tape seconds, original transcript page and stamp, direction of transmission, audible landmark and pick uncertainty. Use a consistent landmark, such as first intelligible speech. Compare neighboring verified rows to estimate timestamp scatter. Inspect uplink and downlink separately for systematic latency before pooling them.
2. **Give each candidate the same training anchors.** Let `t` be the reference-equivalent post-05b time of the main utterance and `Q(t)` the integrated stage-06 map. Compare offset alone (`a + t`), the Quindar map with a fitted mission origin (`a + Q(t)`), and constant transcript-derived rate (`a + b·t`). Also test `a + b·Q(t)` if transcript residuals suggest that Quindar tracks local variation but has an overall rate bias. Fit robustly, weighting observations by timing uncertainty, including uncertainty in channel stagger. If eligible Quindar evidence is insufficient, report that comparison as unavailable. One secure call establishes a local offset. Widely separated calls constrain rate.
3. **Score predictions in seconds.** Plot predicted GET minus printed GET against mission time for each candidate. Mark held-out exchanges, uncertainty, tape boundaries and spans with few tones or calls. Report held-out median absolute error, largest absolute error, signed early-to-late drift and time span tested. Separately plot PAO-to-tape and DVD-to-tape interval differences within continuous segments. A step confined to one reference warrants inspection for an edit. A trend across transcript-verified calls on several tape channels warrants testing a tape-rate change. A constant bias common to all transcript times is unobservable with this evidence.
4. **Choose the simplest map whose improvement exceeds the timing uncertainty on held-out calls.** If the Quindar curve predicts them within the transcript scatter, retain it and estimate the mission origin. If a constant stretch improves predictions, report the fitted rate and the seconds gained or lost over the tested span. Consider a smoothly changing rate only when multiple independent exchanges support it on both sides and it improves another withheld region. Treat a confirmed source splice as a segment boundary. Keep unsupported stretches provisional and show where absolute placement becomes uncertain.

## Apply the same long-baseline test to PAO and DVD audio

Fit a source-time-to-GET map for each continuous PAO transfer and each continuous portion of a DVD title. Start each with its own offset and rate. Multiple channels from the same transfer can contribute evidence for that transfer. After the PAO pilot, look for the same EVA-2 calls on T510093 and in a continuous DVD segment. Pick the corresponding local time independently on each source and fit each to the original transcript. A clear PAO recording can identify a weak DVD utterance. Both observations then trace back to the same printed GET with the same timestamp uncertainty.

A chapter boundary is a place to check continuity. A verified cut, inserted commentary sequence or replay needs its own treatment. Keep DVD program time available for playback and record the original event GET separately for replays. Use the accepted audio timing map for corresponding video presentation times if producing a mission-timed video edition. A short segment with one verified call supports placement with uncertainty. Mark its rate unresolved until widely separated events or demonstrable transfer continuity constrain it.

**First review deliverable.** For PAO reel 572-AAA, produce one plot of timing error through the recording, an anchor table with original page references, and the supported GET interval and held-out error for each candidate map. In the follow-up comparison, add separate panels for the 30-track and DVD fits. Check the 30-track map on a second channel and test overlapping calls on T510092 using that reel’s own map. A future 30-track render would use one accepted map per tape for every channel, preserving measured origins and original audio.
